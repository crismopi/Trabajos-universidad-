### 4.7.2 Site Access

The project site will be accessed via a single main gate opening directly onto Ndwaru Road. This access point is strategically positioned to ensure safe visibility and turning radius for both inbound and outbound vehicles. The main entry and exit lanes will be clearly demarcated and surfaced with cabro paving, allowing for the segregation of vehicular and pedestrian movements.

The access design incorporates:

- Acceleration and deceleration lanes along the frontage to reduce vehicular conflict during entry and exit;
- Security gatehouse and control barrier to manage traffic flow and enhance safety;
- Pedestrian walkway integrated along the frontage, connecting to the adjacent developments; and
- Raised zebra crossing and rumble strips recommended across Ndwaru Road at the entrance to slow approaching traffic and safeguard pedestrians.

The project is well-served by the existing transport network and does not require major off-site road upgrading beyond localized frontage improvements and safety features. Coordination with KURA will be undertaken to implement minor access realignments and install signage consistent with urban traffic safety standards.

### 4.7.3 Traffic Assessment

The Traffic Impact Assessment conducted on September 2025² for the Keza–Riruta corridor, which comprising the Riruta IHS site, provides the baseline and forecast traffic conditions applicable to the proposed development. The study was undertaken in accordance with the applicable standards³, adjusted to reflect Nairobi’s urban mobility context and local vehicle ownership patterns.

**a) Baseline Traffic Conditions**

Traffic surveys were carried out at key intersections along Ndwaru and Naivasha Roads during weekday (Friday and Monday) and weekend (Saturday) peak periods. The data covered morning (6:00–9:00 a.m.), midday (12:00–3:00 p.m.), and evening (4:00–7:00 p.m.) peaks.

Findings indicated that:

- Current traffic operations along Ndwaru Road and the immediate access points function at acceptable Levels of Service during all peak periods.
- The Naivasha Road–Ndwaru Road junction experiences moderate congestion during the morning peak, attributed to turning movements from Naivasha Road northbound traffic.
- Pedestrian activity along Ndwaru Road is high, though sidewalks are narrow and occasionally encroached, forcing pedestrians to share the carriageway with vehicles.
- Existing speeds are occasionally excessive, necessitating traffic calming and enhanced signage for pedestrian safety.

These baseline findings confirm that Ndwaru Road currently operates within capacity, with adequate provision for motorized and non-motorized users, though localized safety and pedestrian infrastructure improvements are warranted.

**b) Future Traffic Projections and Trip Generation**

The TIA projected traffic growth to Year 2027, factoring in both background traffic increase of 5% annual growth rate, and additional trips generated by the proposed development.
