L285: IHS has been an IFC client since 2012 where IFC invested in IHS South Africa Workforce Housing Fund II (“IHS Fund II”, cite47†https://disclosures.ifc.org/project-detail/SII/31851/ihssa-project ) split into a two sleeves – one for investments in South Africa and a second sleeve for investments in Namibia and Botswana, which focused on green affordable housing investments. L286: The proposed investment is an equity co-investment package of up to €20m along with IHS Kenya Green Housing Partnership LLP and IHS Kenya Green Housing SCSp (the “Main Fund”) in the development and acquisition of approximately 10,800 newly developed, resource efficient, “green” affordable housing properties in Nairobi and other major urban areas in Kenya (the “Project”).
L287: The proposed equity investment will consist of €10m from IFC for its own account and up to US$10m equivalent in € from IFC acting as the implementing entity of the UKIFC Market Accelerator for Green Construction Program (MAGC), the UK-IFC bilateral partnership in blended concessional finance for climate change mitigation.
L288: Instead of investing directly in the Main Fund, IFC (and blended finance) will utilize a “sidecar” investment structure by using a newly created Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) (the “ManageCo”) with IFC as the only limited partner that will co-invest alongside with the Fund in selected investments. The Main Fund will be managed by IHS Kenya Green Housing Fund GP Limited (the “General Partner” or the “GP”).
L289: Also, the Main Fund has outsourced investment management to IHS (Kenya) Limited, a company domiciled in Kenya (referred to as “IHS Kenya”).
L290: IHS Kenya has developed a strong pipeline of development properties and links with developers who have promising pipeline and land opportunities. The developments will typically be around 350 units of modest size (e.g., two-bed one-bath at <55m²) in a secure estate with on-site amenities (e.g., playgrounds and open areas), with close access to schools, retail, transportation, and work opportunities. There are two development models expected to be followed by IHS Kenya:
L291: (a)   A portion of these developments will be acquired on a turnkey basis i.e. where a development partner constructs and is paid for the units once complete. IHS Kenya will sign a turn-key agreement with the developers/sellers; and
L292: 
L293: (b)   The other properties will be developed under a fixed price contract with contractor and where IHS will manage the construction. IHS Kenya will hire a project management company to monitor construction on their behalf.
L294: All developments will comply with IFC EDGE standard requiring minimum 20% savings on water usage, energy usage and embodied energy (production of materials). Once completed and transferred, some of the units shall be rented, others sold and an additional portion shall have the option on being in the Tenant Purchase Scheme rent-to-buy product, though this can be varied based on market conditions.
L295: The properties will be managed by IHS Property Management (IHSPM), the property management company belonging to IHS. L9363: # AfCFTA Market Requirements
L9364: 
L9365: AfCFTA framework for Kenyan exporters
L9366: The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) brings together fifty-five countries of the African Union and the Regional Economic Communities to create a single continental market. The Agreement entered into force on 30 May 2019 and trading commenced on 1 January 2021. Kenya signed on 21 March 2018 and was among the first Member States to deposit its instruments of ratification, on 10 May 2018, and it participates in the pilot phase of the Guided Trade Initiative.
L9367: For an exporter, the AfCFTA is a set of instruments to be worked through for each product and each destination.
L9368: 
L9369: Tariffs. Each State Party applies its own schedule of concessions, so the rate is not uniform across the continent. The cite4076†AfCFTA e-Tariff Book†etariff.au-afcfta.org gives the applicable rate, phase-down category and corresponding rules of origin once the exporting country, importing country and HS code are entered.
L9370: Rules of origin. Preference applies only to goods that qualify as originating. Consignments of USD 5,000 and above require a cite4077†Certificate of Origin , issued in Kenya by the Rules of Origin Section of the Kenya Revenue Authority; alternatively an Approved Exporter may make an Origin Declaration on the invoice or another commercial document. A proof of origin is valid for twelve months from issue.
L9371: Small packages between private persons (up to USD 500) and goods in a traveller's personal luggage (up to USD 1,200) need no proof of origin.
L9372: Other requirements. Prohibited and restricted goods lists are set nationally and must be checked for the destination market. An advance ruling from the importing country's customs administration settles tariff classification and origin before shipment, and is worth obtaining before a first consignment.
L9373: Where a trade obstacle arises, the non-tariff barrier mechanism under Annex 5 allows the private sector to file a complaint for resolution within set timelines, and a Dispute Settlement Mechanism operates between State Parties.
L9374: Conformity determines whether the goods can enter the market at all; the destination's tariff offer and the origin status of the goods determine only the rate they pay on entry. A consignment that does not qualify as originating still moves, but at the full MFN rate.
L9375: The five priority products and their target markets. To analyse the market requirements under this framework, five priority Kenyan export products were identified: palm oil (HS 1511), corrugated cartons (HS 4819), luminaires and lighting fittings (HS 9405), air conditioners (HS 8415) and fertilizer (HS Chapter 31).
L9376: Kenya's exports of all five already move almost entirely within its established preferential neighbourhood, East African Community Customs Union and COMESA markets, where the goods already enter duty-free under an older instrument. Each product was therefore analysed against an illustrative destination lying outside both blocs, reached only under the AfCFTA regime and selected for the size or structure of its demand for that product.
L9377: This tests genuine AfCFTA access rather than trade already covered by existing preferences, and responds to the concentration revealed in the trade data, where a single neighbouring market often takes a third or more of a product's exports.

L9435: For inorganic and mineral fertilizers, KEBS product certification and the applicable Standardization Mark are required for exports to EAC Partner States, based on the relevant KS 158 and KS 2290 series standards, supported by product testing and composition analysis. A certificate of analysis from an accredited laboratory and the applicable radioanalysis certification are also required.
L9436: For organic and bio-fertilizers, KEPHIS exporter registration, risk assessment and phytosanitary controls apply, including the relevant product inspection and certification.
L9437: Regulatory requirements in Benin. The product standard is regional: ECOWAS Regulation C/REG.13/12/12 harmonises fertilizer quality control across all fifteen member states, overseen since 2023 by the West African Committee for Fertilizer Control, so a formulation compliant for one ECOWAS market is substantially compliant for Benin, but the national authorisations are not transferable.
L9438: Three separate approvals must be in place before any product moves: product registration or homologation, which requires the full technical data sheet, guaranteed composition, source materials, directions and precautions, supported by bio-efficacy and safety test reports from an approved research centre, and which takes weeks to months; approval of the importer as a licensed fertilizer dealer, including an environmental authorisation for the storage facility; and a prior import authorisation for each consignment, tied to the pro-forma invoice and time-boxed to the shipment window.
L9439: The fertilizer itself is judged on declared nutrient content against the regulation's permitted tolerances over-formulation fails as surely as under-formulation together with heavy-metal limits and physical quality, where moisture is the most common failure in humid coastal handling. Ammonium nitrate and similar high-nitrogen materials are treated as dangerous goods and attract additional security controls.
L9440: Labelling is part of the legal standard: the guaranteed analysis, product name and grade, net weight, manufacturer or responsible importer, batch identification, country of origin and directions for use must match the certificate of analysis and the registration exactly, and Benin is francophone, so labels and accompanying documents should be in French.
L9441: Conformity assessment and customs at destination. Benin verifies imports through its pre-shipment verification programme, operated by Benin Control SA, which produces the documentary verification attestation used to establish customs value and, for a regulated product such as fertilizer, triggers sampling and laboratory analysis against the ECOWAS tolerance table, with public capacity at the soil, water and environment laboratory in Cotonou.
L9442: The clearance file comprises the importer card, the cargo tracking note (BESC/ECTN), which is mandatory for all cargo discharged at Cotonou and must be raised before shipment with its number appearing on the bill of lading, the prior import authorisation, commercial invoice and packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin and the certificate of analysis or conformity.
L9443: A phytosanitary certificate is required for organic fertilizers and soil amendments of plant or animal origin, but generally not for mineral or synthetic grades. The two structural bottlenecks are the front-loaded registration and authorisation before the goods move, and the destination laboratory analysis on arrival, which runs from several days to two weeks and is the single most common cause of demurrage.
L9444: The tariff is not the constraint: shipping with a credible manufacturer's certificate of analysis, ideally supported by an independent pre-shipment conformity test, so that the destination analysis merely confirms the paperwork, removes the largest variable in the chain.
