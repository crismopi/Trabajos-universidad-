L2@P0-1: A DELIBERATE CHOICEii 5th Kenya Human Development Report
L3@P1: This is the fifth National Human Development Report for Kenya. It presents an independent voice on the interconnectedness of human security and human development. Though sponsored by UNDP, the report does not reflect UN or UNDP
L4@P1: policy. It is a rejoinder to the unprecedented promise by World leaders to address as a single package, peace,security,
L5@P1: development, human rights and fundamental freedoms. As articulated in the Secretary General’s report “In Larger
L6@P1: Freedom:towards development, security and human rights for all”, “We will not enjoy development without security, we
L7@P1: will not enjoy security without development, and we will not enjoy either without respect for human rights. Unless all
L8@P1: these causes are advanced, none will succeed”.
L9@P1: UNDP-sponsored Human Development Reports over the years have won a well-deserved reputation for excellence. They
L10@P1: have played an indispensable catalytic role in helping shape and forge concrete responses to key policy debates. As this
L11@P1: report persuasively argues, one of the challenges facing community development in Kenya is that of controlling human
L12@P1: insecurity, which challenges the efforts towards achieving the Millennium Development Goals by the target date of 2015.
L13@P1: This agenda will not succeed unless we can decisively resolve the bottlenecks retarding progress in the key realm of human
L14@P1: security. The report looks at the facts and delivers a compelling and comprehensive analysis on how this can be done. It
L15@P1: suggests ways and means in which the country and its leadership can take concrete action to address deficits in security,
L16@P1: to safeguard Kenya from the threat of want and fear so that every man, woman and child in the street can have the freedom to live a life of dignity.
L17@P1: It is my hope that, as an articulation of a key development challenge, this report will frame an ambitious agenda for Kenya
L18@P1: in the months and years to come.
L19@P1: Paul Andre de la Porte
L20@P1: Resident Representative, UNDP, Kenya.

L317@P7: The Concept and Framework of Human Security
L318@P7: This report examines how Human Security and Human
L319@P7: Development are mutually reinforcing anchoring the concept
L320@P7: on UNDP’s expanded definition as articulated in the 1994
L321@P7: Global Human Development Report. At the outset, the report
L322@P7: observes that for a large majority of Kenyans, the broader
L323@P7: dimensions of human security is a novelty since they have till
L324@P7: now all along embraced security in the traditional sense.
L325@P7: The expanded human security concept was introduced by
L326@P7: UNDP in 1994 in an attempt to move away from the narrow
L327@P7: traditional security focus hinged on the protection and military defence from threats to territorial integrity and sovereignty of states. It expanded the frontiers to include a comprehensive coverage of other factors that, perhaps even
L328@P7: more significantly, threaten life, survival, dignity and the well
L329@P7: being of individuals.
L330@P7: This broader concept equates security with people rather
L331@P7: than territories, with development rather than arms. It
L332@P7: visualizes that without human security, there is limited
L333@P7: chance for people to enhance their capabilities and expand
L334@P7: freedoms by removing barriers to development, livelihoods
L335@P7: and sources of unfreedom - such as poverty, poor economic
L336@P7: opportunities, social deprivation or absence of institutions
L337@P7: for the maintenance of peace and security. It places human
L338@P7: security threats under seven main clusters:- economic
L339@P7: security, food security, health security, environmental security, personal security, community security, and political
L340@P7: security. This definition provides elements that are so concrete that,when considered together, provide a powerful
L341@P7: conceptual tool for policy and action to move forward the
L342@P7: cause of human development and welfare.
L343@P7: In that vein, human security permits progress on human
L344@P7: development by providing the essence (a) to live a long,
L345@P7: healthy and productive life (b) to access knowledge (c) to
L346@P7: have sufficient material to meet basic needs (d) to participate freely in community and public life. Guaranteed security reassures progress on several fronts, namely (i) strong
L347@P7: growth, sustainable and equality of wealth (ii) development
L348@P7: of human health and education (iii) fight against poverty
L349@P7: (iv) creation of decent jobs (vi) support to women and
L350@P7: advancement of gender equality (vii) taking care of the
L351@P7: environment, and (viii) promotion of good governance, both
L352@P7: political, economic, social and cultural.
L353@P7: Despite the critical linkage between human security and
L354@P7: human development, Kenya has not explicitly embraced the
L355@P7: concept of human security in her development approaches.
L356@P7: The State of Human Development
L357@P7: Kenya’s Human Development Index has increased marginally in recent years, reflecting people’s improving standards
L358@P7: of living from rising economic growth. Nevertheless, a
L359@P7: majority of Kenyans are still trapped in deepening poverty.
L360@P7: The Human Poverty Index for 2005 is 37 percent but income
L361@P7: poverty levels are higher at 56 percent. Other important
L362@P7: messages are as follows:
L363@P7: • Kenya’s HDI has increased from 0.520 in 2004 to 0.532
L364@P7: in 2005 maintaining Kenya in the Medium human
L365@P7: development level. This has largely resulted from the
L366@P7: benefits of growth in the economy and increasing access
L367@P7: to educational and health facilities.
L368@P7: • Regions within the country with low HDI and GDI
L369@P7: coincide with those encountering persistent human
L370@P7: insecurity in form of conflict and disasters such as
L371@P7: floods, low potential as in ASAL areas and those that
L372@P7: suffer degradation.
L373@P7: • Regions with high human development are those that
L374@P7: offer higher opportunities and with relatively better
L375@P7: human security.
L376@P7: • Women empowerment and participation in economic,
L377@P7: professional (administrative, managerial and technical)
L378@P7: and political/elective spheres, although improving, still
L379@P7: remains very low.
L380@P7: A significant implication of the above is that without guaranteed safety from personal harm and threats to life and
L381@P7: property, human beings cannot engage in development
L382@P7: activities that enrich their lives and enable them to conquer adversities such as hunger and poverty. As a fundamental platform for the human development process,
L383@P7: human security lays emphasis on human rights and opening up resources and livelihood opportunities to people. It
L384@P7: anchors on the entrenchment of the rule of law and legal
L385@P7: and judicial systems that deepen concerns for human
L386@P7: rights in the country and safeguarding people from underlying causes of human insecurity that include economic
L387@P7: despair and social injustice.
L388@P7: It also includes providing people with secure, productive and
L389@P7: satisfying lives and deepened processes and systems that
L390@P7: give people chances for survival, livelihood and dignity.
L391@P7: National Responses to Human Insecurity
L392@P7: Kenya has pursued a myriad of policy regimes, legislative and
L393@P7: legal frameworks and programmatic interventions aimed at
L394@P7: reducing human insecurity and improving livelihoods.
L395@P7: However, to a large extent, these efforts have directly sought
L396@P7: economic development. Access to education, health, property rights, social security, freedom and political participation, equality and non-discrimination have over time been
L397@P7: seen from a purely economic perspective.
L398@P7: By implication, economic development has been viewed
L399@P7: as the ultimate goal and achievement of human development as a residual. One of the main reasons has been
L400@P7: that the concepts of human security and human development had not become part of policy discourse before
L401@P7: 1990. In recent times, Kenya has instituted efforts aimed
L402@P7: at addressing human security and human development
L403@P7: including addressing fear-based insecurity, gender basedinsecurity, terror threats, as well as want-based insecurity.
L404@P7: Such efforts include major policy articulations such as
L405@P7: NPEP, PRSP, ERS, Police Act and Police reforms including
L406@P7: community policing, Safer Cities Programme, GJLOS,
